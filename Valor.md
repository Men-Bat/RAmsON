## SECTION C.7: EUROPEAN ADDED VALUE & POLICY ALIGNMENT

### C.7.1. Why is cross-border cooperation essential to achieve the project's objectives? (¿Por qué es esencial la cooperación transfronteriza?)

[TEXT TO INSERT:]
The survival of the critically endangered Balkan Lynx (Lynx lynx balcanicus) cannot be achieved through isolated national initiatives. With fewer than 50 individuals remaining in the wild, the species' home ranges span across the highly fragmented mountainous borders of North Macedonia, Albania, and Kosovo. 

By design, this project establishes a unified ecosystem management approach that renders national administrative borders obsolete. Fragmented national actions would lead to disjointed monitoring efforts and inconsistent protection laws, inadvertently accelerating the species' extinction. Localizing the innovative "WarNet" stratospheric monitoring grid and deploying the "Mini AiR Fly" sub-drones requires a coordinated cross-border telecommunications and airspace protocol. 

Furthermore, transferring the institutional knowledge and successful captive breeding frameworks developed by the Spanish Ministry for Ecological Transition to the Balkan region represents the highest form of European horizontal cooperation. Bringing together MES (North Macedonia), PPNEA (Albania), and Finch/ERA (Kosovo) under a shared technological and environmental banner is the only viable pathway to ensure seamless genetic connectivity along the Dinaric-Mediterranean corridor down to Vlorë.

### C.7.2. How does the project contribute to wider European Union strategies and policies? (¿Cómo contribuye el proyecto a las estrategias de la UE?)

[TEXT TO INSERT:]
The "Balkan Lynx Sky-Corridor & Recovery Initiative" directly aligns with and advances three core pillars of the European Union's strategic environmental framework:

1. EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030: The project contributes directly to the overarching target of protecting at least 30% of Europe’s land and sea areas. By utilizing the non-invasive, solar-powered "WarNet" aerial platforms, the project maps and safeguards pristine primary forests in the Mavrovo-Shebenik axis, accelerating the formal establishment and delineation of future Emerald and Natura 2000 networks in candidate member states.

2. The Green Agenda for the Western Balkans: Through the deployment of 100% emission-free, solar-powered High Altitude platforms (WarNet), the project strictly adheres to the "Do No Significant Harm" (DNSH) principle and the decarbonization targets of the European Green Deal. The project demonstrates that cutting-edge, high-tech wildlife conservation can be accomplished with zero greenhouse gas emissions, serving as a flagship model for the region's ecological transition.

3. EU Strategy for the Adriatic and Ionian Region (EUSAIR): The extension of the ecological corridor to the maritime zones of Vlorë (Vjosa-Narta Protected Landscape and Karaburun Peninsula) directly strengthens EUSAIR’s Pillar 3 (Environmental Quality). The project successfully addresses the critical issue of habitat fragmentation caused by macro-infrastructures—such as the new Vlorë International Airport (VIA) and coastal tourism developments—by implementing mandatory, legally binding biodiversity mitigation protocols, ecological bridges, and sound-absorbing forestry belts financed through EU funds.

### C.7.3. What are the permanent benefits and structures that will remain after the project's EU funding ends? (¿Qué beneficios permanentes perdurarán al finalizar los fondos?)

[TEXT TO INSERT:]
The project is built on a strict foundation of structural, economic, and technological sustainability that guarantees self-sufficiency beyond the EU funding lifecycle (2026-2032):

* Institutional Infrastructure: The newly constructed Transboundary Captive Breeding Center in Mavrovo will remain as a permanent state-of-the-art scientific institution, managed by local ecological societies and fully integrated into the national budgets of the beneficiary countries as a permanent public asset.

* Technological Autonomy and Local Capabilities: The procurement model ensures that while the core patented technology of "WarNet" and "Mini AiR Fly" is sourced from experienced Spanish partners, the system assembly, daily flight operations, and AI data maintenance are entirely transferred to local companies in Albania and North Macedonia. This leaves behind a highly skilled, technologically advanced "green workforce" capable of maintaining the monitoring grid independently.

* Economic Self-Sufficiency via the "WarNet Eco-Certified" Brand: By the end of the project, the socio-economic integration of the corridor will be fully self-funded through the commercial revenues generated by the certified ecoturism network in Vlorë. The transition of former local hunters into tech-supported park rangers and scientific mountain guides will create a permanent, market-driven economic loop, ensuring that the protection of the Balkan Lynx remains a highly profitable and respected endeavor for the local communities indefinitely.
